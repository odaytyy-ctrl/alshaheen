Privacy Policy

ALSHAHEEN is a MetaTrader 5 analysis and trading terminal for Windows. It runs on your own computer, and the developer receives none of your trading data. The only data a copy bought on Whop sends out is its licence check, described in section 05.

01Who this policy is from

ALSHAHEEN (“we”, “us”) is published and operated by an individual developer trading under that name. This policy covers the ALSHAHEEN desktop application, whether it came from the Microsoft Store or was bought on Whop (whop.com), and nothing else. Where the two copies differ, this policy says which one it means.

02What the app reads

ALSHAHEEN does not ask you for personal details, and it has no sign-up or login of its own; a copy bought on Whop asks only for its licence key (section 05). It reads information from the MetaTrader 5 terminal installed and signed in on the same computer, using MetaQuotes’ own Python interface. That information is:

  • your broker account number, the broker’s name, the trade server name and the account currency;
  • account balance, equity, margin, free margin and leverage;
  • your open positions — ticket, volume, prices, stop loss, take profit and floating profit — and the account’s recent deal history;
  • instrument prices, spreads, candle and tick history, and the broker’s list of instruments with their contract specifications.

MetaTrader 5 hands over the whole account record at once, and that record also contains the account holder’s name. ALSHAHEEN does not use that name and does not keep or write it anywhere.

To find the terminal, the app looks at the names of the programs running on your computer and at the program folders on your disk. If no terminal is running, it may start the one you installed. So that it can read their prices, it may add the instruments you use to MetaTrader 5’s Market Watch list.

All of it is read locally, repeatedly while the app runs — every few seconds — and used to draw the interface, to size and check the trades you place or arm, and to measure your exposure. Some of it is written into the files listed in section 03. None of it is transmitted to us.

03What the app writes, and where

ALSHAHEEN keeps working files on your own machine so it can show your history and resume after a restart. When installed from the Microsoft Store it uses two folders: a data folder, %LOCALAPPDATA%\ALSHAHEEN, and a log folder inside it, %LOCALAPPDATA%\ALSHAHEEN\logs. Because ALSHAHEEN is a Microsoft Store app, Windows may keep these folders in the app’s own private storage instead (under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\), where they are removed together with the app. A copy bought on Whop, unpacked from its ZIP file, uses a data folder and a logs folder next to ALSHAHEEN.exe; if the folder it was unpacked into cannot be written to, it uses the two %LOCALAPPDATA% folders above instead. A new version comes as a new ZIP file; unpacked to the same place as the old version, it leaves both folders, and the saved licence key in them, as they are (the Terms of Use, section 05, say how).

04The connections the app makes

Stated plainly

While the link to your broker is down, ALSHAHEEN checks every few seconds whether your own internet is working. It opens a plain TCP connection to 8.8.8.8:53 (Google Public DNS) and, only if that fails, to 1.1.1.1:53 (Cloudflare). No data is sent and no query is made — the connection is opened and closed. As with any internet connection, the operator of those addresses can see that your IP address contacted them.

A copy from the Microsoft Store makes no other connection of its own: its subscription check in section 05 is carried out by Windows. A copy bought on Whop makes one more: the licence check in section 05, an encrypted HTTPS connection to the ALSHAHEEN licence server. The links to these terms and this policy open in your web browser, the Subscribe button opens the Microsoft Store app, and the GET A KEY ON WHOP button opens Whop’s website in your browser, only when you click them. The connection to your broker is opened by the MetaTrader 5 terminal, not by ALSHAHEEN, and is governed by your broker’s and MetaQuotes’ own terms.

05Your subscription

ALSHAHEEN is sold as a subscription in two places, and each copy checks its subscription in its own way.

A copy from the Microsoft Store

Through the Microsoft Store, ALSHAHEEN is sold as a monthly subscription. Microsoft handles the entire purchase. Through the standard Windows.Services.Store interface the app asks Windows whether this copy currently holds an active subscription and when it ends, and — if none is found — whether your Microsoft account has ever owned it. It asks once after each start and again when you press Check again.

We never see, receive or store your name, payment card, billing address or Microsoft account. Purchases and refunds are covered by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.

A successful answer is cached locally in license_state.json, so the app keeps working for up to 30 days if the Store cannot be reached. That file holds the time of the last successful check, its result and the add-on’s Store ID — nothing about you.

A copy from the Microsoft Store does not make the Whop licence check below. The program leaves it out only when it is running as ALSHAHEEN’s own Microsoft Store package; any other copy asks for a licence key.

A copy bought on Whop

On Whop (whop.com), ALSHAHEEN is sold as a monthly or yearly subscription. Each copy needs a licence key, which Whop gives you with your order, and the key is bound to one computer.

Stated plainly

A copy bought on Whop checks its licence when it starts, when you press ACTIVATE in its key window, and while it runs: about every 6 hours, and more often while it cannot get an answer (the Terms of Use, section 05, say exactly when). Each check sends these four things over an encrypted HTTPS connection to the ALSHAHEEN licence server (a Cloudflare Worker at alshaheen-license.odaytyy.workers.dev), and nothing else about you or your computer:

  • your licence key;
  • a code that identifies this computer: a one-way SHA-256 hash of a fixed label together with Windows’ MachineGuid (an identifier Windows keeps in its registry), shortened to 32 characters. It holds no name and cannot be turned back into the MachineGuid, but it is pseudonymous, not anonymous: it is the same at every start on the same computer, which is what lets the key be bound to it, and it is stored with your Whop membership, so it is linked to your Whop account. Reinstalling or resetting Windows, or cloning it, can change the MachineGuid, and this code with it;
  • the application’s name, ALSHAHEEN;
  • its version number.

The program sends no trading data, no account number, no name and no e-mail address. Cloudflare, which hosts the licence server, ends the encrypted connection on its own servers, so it handles the whole request: the four things above, your IP address and the request’s standard headers, which name ALSHAHEEN and its version and nothing about you. The licence server runs there too, so Whop’s reply about your membership (below) is handled on Cloudflare’s servers as well. The server’s own code does not read your IP address, keeps no database and writes no log; Cloudflare may keep its own records of what it handles, under Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

The licence server asks Whop about the membership your key belongs to. Whop’s reply holds the membership’s own details — among them its product, its status, the end of its paid period and any computer code stored with it — and can also include your Whop user id, your Whop username and a phone number, if you gave Whop one at checkout or have shared one with us as the seller on Whop. It does not include your e-mail address. The server bases its answer only on the product, the status and the stored computer code, and keeps nothing from the reply. The program receives only that answer: yes, with the membership’s status and the end of its paid period; or no, with the reason, and the status when the membership is not active. The first time a key is used, the server stores the computer code with that membership at Whop; that is what binds the key to one computer, and every later check compares the code it receives with the stored one. Apart from any records Cloudflare keeps (above), the only thing the check leaves outside your computer is that computer code, stored with your membership at Whop.

On your computer the program keeps the result in licence.json, in its data folder (section 03): the key, the computer code, the time of the last successful check, the status and the paid-until date Whop reported, the reason if the key was refused, and the latest time it has read from the clock. That lets it start without asking for the key again, keep working for up to 24 hours without internet after the last successful check, and notice a clock that was moved back. When the subscription is no longer active the whole program stops; the Terms of Use say exactly when.

Your purchase and payment are handled by Whop, under Whop’s own privacy policy. The program never asks for and never sends any payment or billing detail. As the seller, we receive from Whop what that policy says Whop may share with a seller: “the confirmation of your purchase, username, contact and certain account profile information”. We use them only to run your subscription and to answer you when you write to us — never for advertising and never for marketing e-mails. We keep them for those two purposes only, and we delete them when you ask (section 08), unless a law requires us to keep them. Whop’s own records of your account and your purchase stay under Whop’s policy.

07Keeping and deleting your data

Your trading data never reaches us, so there is none of it on our side to retain, export or erase. Everything the app writes lives on your machine for as long as you keep it. For a copy bought on Whop, the computer code is stored with your membership at Whop, we hold the purchase details Whop passes to us as the seller, which we delete when you ask unless a law requires us to keep them (section 08), and Cloudflare may keep its own records of the licence checks (section 05). Whop’s own records of your account and your purchase fall under Whop’s privacy policy.

To remove a copy from the Microsoft Store: uninstall ALSHAHEEN from Windows Settings. If Windows kept the app’s files in its private storage, they are removed with it; otherwise delete the %LOCALAPPDATA%\ALSHAHEEN folder, which holds both folders. A copy bought on Whop is not installed, so Windows Settings does not list it: it runs from the folder you unpacked it into. Delete that folder, which holds its data folder (with licence.json in it) and its logs folder, and %LOCALAPPDATA%\ALSHAHEEN if it used that instead. If you moved the folders with the environment variables in section 03, delete those folders too. Deleting a copy bought on Whop does not remove the computer code stored with your membership at Whop, or the purchase details we hold: reset your licence key from your Whop orders page, or write to the address in section 12 to have the code cleared and the purchase details deleted. Your broker and MetaQuotes keep their own records of your account and your orders, under their own policies.

08Your rights

Data-protection law gives you the right to access, correct, export and erase personal data a company holds about you. For a copy from the Microsoft Store we hold none, so those rights are satisfied by design rather than by request — every file the app writes is listed in section 03 and is on your own computer. For a copy bought on Whop, we hold the purchase details Whop passes to us as the seller, and the computer code is stored with your membership at Whop (section 05). Write to the address in section 12 to ask for a copy of what we hold, to have the purchase details deleted — unless a law requires us to keep them — or to have the computer code cleared. Whop’s own records of your account and your purchase are covered by Whop’s privacy policy, and any records Cloudflare keeps of the licence checks by Cloudflare’s; both companies may handle data outside your own country. You can also complain to the data-protection authority where you live.

09Children

ALSHAHEEN is financial software for adults and is not directed at children. It is not intended for anyone under 18. Apart from the licence check of a copy bought on Whop and the purchase details Whop passes to us as the seller (section 05), nothing is collected from anyone of any age.

10Security

ALSHAHEEN never sees and never stores your broker password — you sign in to MetaTrader 5 yourself. It does write your account number and trade server name into the files listed in section 03; treat those files as private. They are ordinary plain-text files, protected by your own Windows user account. In a copy bought on Whop, licence.json also holds your licence key in readable form: its seal shows whether it was edited and does not hide what is in it, so keep it as private as the key. The licence check itself travels over HTTPS, and the program verifies the server’s certificate before it sends anything.

06Risk

Stated plainly

Trading carries risk of loss. Money you place in the market can be lost, and you can lose all of it. Only trade money you can afford to lose entirely.

The instruments ALSHAHEEN can be pointed at are typically leveraged. Positions are opened on margin, so a small move against you can cost a large share of the money committed to that trade. Whether a loss can exceed the money in your account is set by your broker’s terms and your own jurisdiction’s rules, not by this application.

The same lot size does not mean the same risk. The stop is placed at twice the recent volatility of the instrument, and the target at three times that distance, measured on the timeframe above the one being traded. Volatility differs between instruments and changes through the day, so a wider stop at the same lot size is a proportionally larger loss when it is hit — several times larger, between one instrument and another, at the same number of lots. If you size by fixed lots, the money at risk moves with the volatility. If you size by risk, the program works the lot out from the stop distance so the money stays where you set it. Choose knowingly.

Past results do not predict future ones. Any figure shown in the application, written in its log, or produced by any test over historical data describes what already happened on particular data. It says nothing about what will happen next, and it is not a projection.

A stop loss is an instruction sent to your broker, not a guarantee of the price you leave at. Gaps, weekends, news, thin books and slippage can close a position worse than the level asked for. A take profit is the same. Spread, swap and commission are your broker’s and are paid on every trade — the spread is paid the moment the trade opens.

No order is guaranteed to reach your broker or to fill at the price you were shown. Rejection, requote, partial fill and slippage are your broker’s, not ours. The application reports what the broker answered, including its own rejection code, and verifies a rejection against the account before believing it.

Nothing in ALSHAHEEN — no signal, no colour, no score, no suggested lot, no exposure verdict — is advice, a recommendation, or a prediction. Every decision to trade is yours.

07Automation, and your responsibility

Read this before the list

One piece of automation is not part of AUTO mode and does not wait to be armed. Whenever ALSHAHEEN is running, it watches every position it opened — including a position you placed by hand through its own order ticket, with no arming and no further question — and once such a trade is far enough ahead it moves that position’s stop loss, at your broker, to a level in profit, and then follows the price at that distance. It never widens a stop and it never closes a position this way. But it does change where your money leaves the market on a trade you did not arm. If you do not want that, do not place the trade through ALSHAHEEN.

ALSHAHEEN also has a mode that places orders on its own. Nothing is armed and no order is sent until you press START AUTO-TRADING. Arming is per instrument and per timeframe: arming one timeframe does not disturb another on the same instrument.

Before anything is armed, the application states in advance, in a dialog you must answer, that from now on, without asking you again, it opens a real position now on any instrument that already has a signal, and on every new candle of that timeframe it opens, and reverses, positions by itself — and that you stop it with STOP AUTO-TRADING, or with Stop selected and Stop ALL in the Slots panel. It shows, in the same dialog, the margin the whole set requires against your real free margin, and what it would cost if every stop loss were hit. If those figures cannot be read from your account, it says so rather than printing a number nobody has.

Once armed, this is what it does. Read it before you arm anything.

  • It acts on closed candles. A reading is taken from candles that have finished, never from the one still forming.
  • It keeps trading after losses. Being stopped out does not disarm anything — an armed instrument goes on opening new positions on later candles. It stops trading by itself only when you stop it, when you close its position by hand, when a take profit is reached, or after a second exit through a stop the profit lock had moved into profit. The last three also hold that instrument back until you arm it again by hand.
  • Disarming never closes a position. It withdraws the program’s permission to open and reverse. An open trade stays open, with its stop and target where they are.
  • It resumes after a restart, without asking — but only for instruments still holding a position it opened itself. Nothing is opened on resume; management restarts from the next candle. Anything that did not resume is named in the log.
  • A separate process can restart the application. A supervisor started alongside it watches a liveness file from outside. If the application wedges while positions are open, the supervisor stops it and starts it again — after three consecutive stale checks, at most three times per session, with a cooldown between attempts and a grace period at startup. If it wedges with no position open, the supervisor raises an alarm and does nothing. Taken with the point above, this means automatic trading can resume with nobody present.
  • A wide spread does not stop a trade. When the spread has widened to at least twice its own recent median, and is wide in absolute terms, the application asks you, with the numbers, and waits 45 seconds. If nobody answers — the ordinary case while it trades and you are away — the order is opened anyway, and the log says so. If it has no usable spread history for that instrument it cannot ask at all, and the order goes through unchecked — the log says that too.
  • Declining a wide spread on a reversal can leave you with no position. A reversal closes the existing position first and asks about the spread second. If you answer “skip this trade” to the question, the old position is already gone and nothing opens in its place. That is the one case where answering the question changes what you are holding.
  • On a disconnect it cannot close anything. A close order cannot reach a broker that is not reachable. What protects an open position then is the stop loss and take profit already sitting on the broker’s server. When the link returns, the application reads what actually happened to each position while it was blind, and re-enters only on a fresh reading, never on stale data.
  • If it crashes or you close it, nothing is closed. No position is touched on exit, the broker-side stops stand, and positions are picked up again on the next start. While it is not running there is no profit lock, no reversal handling and no exposure warning.
  • Trades you open by hand in MetaTrader 5 are invisible to it. It sees only the positions it opened itself, and it never manages, reverses or closes anything else.
Whose order it is

Every order AUTO mode places is your own order, in your own account, with your own broker. You chose the instrument, the timeframe and the size, and you armed it. You can disarm it at any time, and you can close any position yourself in MetaTrader 5 whether ALSHAHEEN is running or not. Leaving it armed and unattended is your decision and your risk.

There is no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting and no user account, and the files it writes stay on your own disk, in a logs folder and a data folder under your own Windows profile.

No credential is ever held. ALSHAHEEN has no login of its own and no field anywhere in it for a broker login, password or server name. You sign in to MetaTrader 5 yourself, and ALSHAHEEN attaches to that already-signed-in terminal. It never sees your credentials, so it cannot store them or lose them.

It does read your account number, balance, equity, margin and leverage from the terminal, because it cannot size a trade or measure your exposure without them. Several of the files it writes on your computer contain the account number and the trade server name — among them the application log and spread_evidence.txt, which records each wide-spread reading so you can put timestamped evidence in front of your broker. The Privacy Policy lists every one of them. All of them stay on your disk.

The only outbound connection the application itself makes is a reachability test opened to 8.8.8.8:53 and 1.1.1.1:53 after the broker link drops, to tell your own internet being down from the broker being unreachable. No data is sent. The connection to your broker is made by MetaTrader 5, not by ALSHAHEEN.
